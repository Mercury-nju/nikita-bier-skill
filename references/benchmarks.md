# Benchmarks

Use these examples as quality targets. The goal is not to copy the wording. The goal is to match the level of diagnosis, compression, and mechanism clarity.

## Benchmark 1: Anonymous Social App

### Prompt

```text
Why does this anonymous social app fail to grow on campus?
```

### Good Shape

```text
Score
Demand 1/2
Orchestration 0/2
Distribution 0/2
Density 1/2
Shareability 0/2
Durability 1/2
Total 3/12

Core diagnosis
This is not failing because it is anonymous. It is failing because the first meaningful social moment cannot be staged on demand.

Why it dies
Users arrive into an empty room. No one is waiting for them, nothing compels a screenshot, and there is no immediate relationship tension.

Smallest fix
Stop treating anonymity as the hook. Build one forced, high-feedback loop that reliably creates social payoff inside a dense campus subgraph.

What not to do
Do not add more posting surfaces before the first payoff is reliable.
```

## Benchmark 2: Useful But Not Shared

### Prompt

```text
This tool is useful, but nobody invites anyone else. Why?
```

### Good Shape

```text
Score
Demand 1/2
Orchestration 2/2
Distribution 0/2
Density 0/2
Shareability 0/2
Durability 2/2
Total 5/12

Core diagnosis
You built utility, not a social situation.

Why it dies
The user gets private value, but nothing in the output creates a reason to forward it, compare it, or talk about it.

Smallest fix
Turn the result into a visible artifact with status, surprise, or comparison value.

What not to do
Do not confuse retention with spread.
```

## Benchmark 3: Launch Strategy

### Prompt

```text
Should we launch broadly or test quietly first?
```

### Good Shape

```text
Core diagnosis
This is an exposure-control problem, not a hype problem.

Why it dies or spreads
If the product needs density before it feels alive, broad exposure too early will generate contaminated negative signal.

Smallest fix
Find the smallest repeated community where you can create value on demand and test there first.

What not to do
Do not mistake broad awareness for valid learning.
```

## Benchmark 4: Messaging Product

### Prompt

```text
Why is messaging such a bad category for small teams?
```

### Good Shape

```text
Core diagnosis
Messaging is infrastructure competition disguised as feature competition.

Why it dies
Users expect perfect delivery, universal reach, and no missing features. Anything less feels broken, not promising.

Smallest fix
Avoid replacing the user's main inbox. Find a narrower wedge where messaging is secondary to a more compelling social object.

What not to do
Do not challenge default chat head-on unless the wedge is overwhelming.
```

## Benchmark 5: Rewriting A Weak Growth Take

### Prompt

```text
Rewrite this growth take so it is sharper and more mechanism-first.
```

### Weak Input

```text
Many products fail because they do not do growth well.
```

### Good Rewrite

```text
Most products do not die because distribution is weak.
They die because the first valuable moment is too hard to produce on demand.
```

## Benchmark Standard

Prefer outputs that:

- name the bottleneck quickly
- separate mechanism from opinion
- recommend one smallest meaningful fix
- avoid generic advice like "market better" or "improve onboarding"
- sound sharp without slipping into parody
