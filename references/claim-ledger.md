# Claim Ledger

This ledger prevents a memorable post from becoming a universal law. A claim is usable only with its precondition and a way it could be wrong. Evidence tiers describe access quality, not causal validity; their definitions are in [casebook.md](casebook.md#evidence-tiers). Full machine transcription remains tier B until independently checked against audio.

| ID | Reconstructed claim | Evidence | Tier | Precondition | What would weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q01 | A product must produce a meaningful human outcome before distribution can be interpreted. | S01, S02, S03 | A/B | The product has a distinct first-value event. | Users repeatedly acquire and retain without that event. |
| Q02 | Faster time to value is useful when it does not remove the state that creates relevance. | S01, S02 | A/B | Setup is not itself the value. | Longer setup materially increases activated use or trust. |
| Q03 | Manual help can reveal maximum appeal but does not prove self-serve demand. | S03 | A | Staff can supply part of the experience. | Users reproduce the result after help is removed. |
| Q04 | Network density can be a prerequisite and a cost of the product. | S04, S12, S16 | A/B/C | Value depends on relevant peers in the same window. | Individual value remains strong with sparse peers. |
| Q05 | Repeated community tests require comparable, independent communities. | S04, S11 | A/B | Multiple communities exist and exposure can be contained. | Communities are porous or only one central graph matters. |
| Q06 | A concentrated launch is a constraint response when the graph must be saturated at once. | S04, S12 | B/C | One community is central and partial exposure is misleading. | A bounded independent test can create the same value state. |
| Q07 | A credible critical path protects the validity of a demand test. | S03, S14, S15, C36 | A | Users can reject the product before considering its value. | A well-executed path still fails with the target user. |
| Q08 | The interesting property of a product may be more valuable than a broad feature set. | C05 | B | The property creates a recognizable value event. | Focused use does not lead to repeat value or qualified acquisition. |
| Q09 | Distribution advantages are temporary and platform-dependent. | S07, S08 | B | The mechanism depends on a platform surface or permission. | The mechanism remains effective across changed constraints. |
| Q10 | Sharing is evidence of distribution only when it produces qualified first value. | S05, S08 | A/B | Shared output can reach a suitable user. | Shares reliably lead to activated users through another path. |
| Q11 | Virality and durability require separate measurements and explanations. | S01, S06, S28, C35 | B/C | The product can be acquired faster than it is retained. | The product's intended use is one-time or naturally seasonal. |
| Q12 | Replacing a main inbox imposes a reliability and reach burden that a narrow messaging feature may not. | S09, S35, C43 | B | Users would move existing conversations. | The feature completes a task without competing with the main inbox. |
| Q13 | Interest-graph activation depends on relevant initial content, not contact import alone. | S10, S20, C27 | B | The product's value is a personalized feed. | Contacts themselves are the valuable content. |
| Q14 | Researching strong existing flows is a product-development input. | S01, C07 | B | The product competes on a familiar interaction or permission. | The comparison product serves a different job or constraint. |
| Q15 | Product integrity includes anticipating spam, manipulation, and unintended use. | S12, S17 | B/C | The system has incentives that can be gamed. | Misuse is implausible or isolated without user impact. |
| Q16 | Temporary products can create real value without becoming durable networks. | S06 | B | The product's promise is compatible with temporary use. | The business depends on long-term retention or habit. |
| Q17 | A platform metric can improve while the underlying human signal worsens. | C12 | B | Ranking or incentives shape what users see. | The metric is directly tied to user-perceived value and remains robust to gaming. |
| Q18 | A product's test geometry determines how much iteration is possible. | S04 | B | Community overlap and density affect exposure. | The product can be tested without network state. |
| Q19 | An interface can suppress preference signals before the ranking system sees them. | S17, C13 | B | The UI blocks or hides the relevant feedback action. | A UI change leaves feedback and qualified exposure unchanged. |
| Q20 | Curated initial content can activate an interest network without relying on contact import. | S17, C14 | B | Specific interests predict valuable people or content. | Packs fail to improve useful first or repeat visits. |
| Q21 | Partial referral growth can complement paid acquisition for a private utility. | S17, C16 | B | Referred users activate and unit economics support paid traffic. | Referrals fail to activate or the combined channel loses money. |
| Q22 | Public previews can expose product constraints before broad release. | S17, C15 | B | Relevant affected users can meaningfully respond. | Feedback misses affected groups or the released mitigation fails. |
| Q23 | A model should state the boundary of effects it can support. | S22, C19 | B | Uncertain downstream effects are deliberately omitted. | Omissions materially reverse the decision or the included effects are inaccurate. |
| Q24 | A portable object's popularity does not by itself establish adoption of its originating app. | S21, C20 | B | Users can circulate or trade the object elsewhere. | Object propagation demonstrably produces qualified app activation and repeat use. |
| Q25 | Product presentation may alter the willingness to send an invitation. | S01, C22 | B | Recommending the product communicates something socially meaningful. | A controlled comparison finds no durable effect on activated referrals. |
| Q26 | Consumer demand and buyer procurement are separate requirements. | S01, S23, C23 | A/B | A popular consumer tool is becoming a licensed institutional product. | Buyer commitment, delivery economics, and repeat revenue are already demonstrated. |
| Q27 | A shared daily completion moment can couple expression, distribution and an external reminder to return. | S19, C25 | B | A satisfying short task creates a comparable, shareable result. | The cadence reduces useful repeat play or shared exposure does not create qualified arrivals/return. |
| Q28 | An exit decision can depend on operating capacity, uncertain hit longevity and the founder's personal stakes. | S19, C26 | B | A founder is choosing between a real offer and continued operation. | Strong sustainable economics, manageable operations and preferred independence justify continuing. |
| Q29 | Recommendations can serve new-author survival as well as viewer consumption. | S20, C28 | B | Unknown authors need relevant exposure and supply contributes to network value. | Exposure fails to create repeat author/reader value or materially degrades the network. |
| Q30 | Conditional asset access can align upside while limiting irreversible cash exposure. | S26, C30 | B | A scarce asset is actually useful and its owner can accept equity and reversion. | Dilution, lock-up costs or unreliable rights exceed the asset's benefit. |
| Q31 | More unsupported behavioral dependencies make a product thesis harder to establish. | S27, C31 | B | Adoption requires several new behaviors without direct evidence. | Existing behavior already supplies the critical steps, or tests establish them. |
| Q32 | Product roles should be assessed through actual craft, decision authority and coordination needs. | S01, C32 | B | Interaction design and handoffs affect zero-to-one consumer work. | Scale, regulation or other work requires coordination that a proposed role change removes. |
| Q33 | Network reputation can survive a rename; a correction near abandonment can reach affected users. | S01, C33 | B | The reputation spreads along the same connected graph and actually causes abandonment. | The network is independent, the reported concern is valid, or the correction does not reduce qualified abandonment. |
| Q34 | Early cash flow supported by credits does not establish recurring economics without them. | S01, C34 | B | Temporary vendor credits materially subsidize operating cost. | Unsubsidized contribution margin and repeated paid value are demonstrated. |
| Q35 | A complementary partnership can justify coordination cost when it unlocks otherwise unavailable user value. | S31, C37 | B | The products create a specific useful combined capability. | Coordination cost exceeds measured incremental value or the same benefit is achievable alone. |
| Q36 | Funding access can favor a less tidy round over waiting on contingent promises. | S30, C38 | B | A first-time founder has limited lead access and a real runway constraint. | A credible lead is available in time or fragmented financing costs exceed its benefit. |
| Q37 | Monetized posting incentives can conflict with platform integrity despite direct API revenue. | S33, C39 | B | Rewards encourage behavior that degrades the network's actual value. | Rewarded behavior adds useful participation without comparable spam or reader costs. |
| Q38 | Author control and public-response rights can optimize different stakeholder outcomes. | S33, C40 | B | The product promises public conversation while exposing authors to hostile replies or quotes. | The proposed control protects users without materially reducing valuable public participation. |
| Q39 | AI assistance and misrepresented human presence impose different integrity questions. | S33, C41 | B | Users rely on the perceived source and independence of expression. | Source expectations do not matter to the use case, or the proposed distinction fails to preserve useful expression and trust. |
| Q40 | A capability inside an existing inbox can have lower migration requirements than replacing it. | S35, S09, C43 | B | The existing graph supports the task and recipients can participate with little added setup. | Recipient friction, platform restrictions or trust costs erase the proposed advantage. |
| Q41 | Confining expansion can preserve iteration before a network's repeat value is established. | S37, C44 | B | Access can be bounded without destroying the value event and premature exposure exhausts the audience. | The graph requires broader activation, or restricting access blocks useful interaction more than it improves learning. |
| Q42 | A retellable origin narrative and the process that produced a product are different evidence. | S37, C45 | B | Public explanation simplifies an iterative process for a team or audience. | Independent contemporaneous records establish that the explanation captures the relevant decision sequence. |

S24 emphasizes repeated shipping as a way to learn what users want. Its categorical opening is not a separate validated law that speed dominates all other constraints. Pair it with S03/S01's requirements for interpretable tests and S22's explicit uncertainty boundary.

## Use rule

When a response relies on a claim, retrieve its case and state the precondition. If no case supplies a precondition or falsifier, present the statement as an open hypothesis rather than as “Nikita's principle.”
