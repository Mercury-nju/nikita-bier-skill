# Reasoning Atlas

This is an evidence map of public decision explanations, not a personality simulation. Its purpose is to reconstruct the tradeoff before recommending a tactic. The variables and procedure below are package-author synthesis; Nikita did not publish this atlas.

## Reconstruct a decision

1. **Identify whose outcome is being optimized.** A player, a viewer, a new author, the whole network, a solo founder, and an investor can prefer different outcomes. Name the decision owner before choosing a metric.
2. **Recover the binding constraint.** Distinguish an explicit constraint in the passage from a plausible one supplied by the analyst. A shortage of relevant accounts is different from a shortage of phone contacts; a cash constraint is different from a bad acquisition valuation.
3. **Separate alternatives actually discussed from alternatives we propose.** Do not manufacture a rejected option or claim he ran an experiment. A public explanation can omit alternatives and outcomes.
4. **Explain the proposed mechanism and its cost.** Include who receives value, who bears the cost, the time horizon, and the state needed for the mechanism to work. For a new-product thesis, trace which behaviors depend on earlier behaviors and distinguish demonstrated prerequisites from unsupported transitions. A long list of assumptions is less informative than locating the weakest required transition; no calibrated transition probabilities are available here.
5. **State what would reverse the recommendation.** Use a relevant observation, not an arbitrary score. If the source supplies no outcome, preserve that gap.

Use the [casebook](casebook.md) for concrete records and the [claim ledger](claim-ledger.md) for conditional mechanisms. A useful reconstruction is `context → competing outcomes → choice → stated reason → observation → unresolved alternative`. Missing links remain missing.

The repeated structure is to identify the constraint and the unit of value, then explain why one tradeoff makes sense in that context. Repeated statements help identify a stable theme. Explicit first-person before/after accounts can support a change of view. Different roles, products or audiences usually support a context distinction; they do not by themselves establish an evolution in belief.

## Learning exercise

Choose a case and hide its choice and explanation. Write the goal, constraints, at least two plausible alternatives and the observation that would change your recommendation. Then reveal the source-backed explanation and compare the mechanisms, not the tone. Record where your own explanation differs and whether the source provides enough evidence to settle it.

For evaluation, previously published cases are learning material. They cannot be recycled as unseen tests after inclusion. See [fidelity-evaluation.md](fidelity-evaluation.md) for the frozen-version pilot and its limits.

## Public material and the service boundary

C45/S37 explicitly shows a gap between a simplified public origin narrative and the iterative process that produced the product. Retain both, compare contemporaneous records, and leave unstated private motives unknown. Do not copy his creative-license position into permission to invent facts in a user's output.

C42/S34 also shows why a free learning package cannot replicate the advertised advisory service: hands-on design, time, access to people and unpublished platform knowledge are outside this corpus. His confidence and client-result claims are interested testimony, not demonstrated prediction accuracy. The skill can teach supported public explanations while its incremental effectiveness remains unproven.

## What is still missing

The public record is not a complete decision log. Prioritize material that supplies an absent link rather than another repetition of a slogan:

- **Failed products:** named alternatives, metrics before closure, costs, the reason to stop, and lessons that survived later attempts. Five's launch description alone supplies none of these outcomes.
- **Commercial decisions:** actual monetization, acquisition terms, buyer constraints and tradeoffs. A reported tactic or a personally meaningful exit does not establish a transferable business model.
- **X tenure:** fuller contemporary records across the entire tenure, responsibility boundaries and experiments rather than later retrospective claims alone.
- **Reliable attribution:** audio verification of bounded multiparty passages, missing replies/media, and access to the full paywalled interview through an ordinary authorized route.
- **Generalization:** fresh cases from different periods/products, repeated independent generations, human review, and product outcomes. Source count has no known conversion into thinking coverage.

Even exhausting discoverable public material cannot identify all private judgments: different internal models can explain the same selected public statements. A bounded reconstruction of documented reasoning can be improved and tested; complete access to a person's mind cannot be certified from this corpus.
