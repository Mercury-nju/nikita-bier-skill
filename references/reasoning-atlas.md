# Reasoning Atlas

This is an evidence map of public decision explanations, not a personality simulation. Its purpose is to reconstruct the tradeoff before recommending a tactic. The variables and procedure below are package-author synthesis; Nikita did not publish this atlas.

## Reconstruct a decision

1. **Identify whose outcome is being optimized.** A player, a viewer, a new author, the whole network, a solo founder, and an investor can prefer different outcomes. Name the decision owner before choosing a metric.
2. **Recover the binding constraint.** Distinguish an explicit constraint in the passage from a plausible one supplied by the analyst. A shortage of relevant accounts is different from a shortage of phone contacts; a cash constraint is different from a bad acquisition valuation.
3. **Separate alternatives actually discussed from alternatives we propose.** Do not manufacture a rejected option or claim he ran an experiment. A public explanation can omit alternatives and outcomes.
4. **Explain the proposed mechanism and its cost.** Include who receives value, who bears the cost, the time horizon, and the state needed for the mechanism to work. For a new-product thesis, trace which behaviors depend on earlier behaviors and distinguish demonstrated prerequisites from unsupported transitions. A long list of assumptions is less informative than locating the weakest required transition; no calibrated transition probabilities are available here.
5. **State what would reverse the recommendation.** Use a relevant observation, not an arbitrary score. If the source supplies no outcome, preserve that gap.

Use the [casebook](casebook.md) for concrete records and the [claim ledger](claim-ledger.md) for conditional mechanisms. A useful reconstruction is `context → competing outcomes → choice → stated reason → observation → unresolved alternative`. Missing links remain missing.

## Recurring tradeoffs

| Decision | Documented preference or explanation | Competing consideration | Transfer condition and limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fast first value vs. setup | Keep setup that creates the graph or content needed for value; S01/S02 | Each step can lose users | Compare meaningful activation and subsequent use, not signup speed alone |
| Small scope vs. credible execution | Make the tested path coherent; S03 | Speed and scarce engineering time | Polish only where poor execution confounds the current demand test |
| Endless play vs. a recurring shared moment | Wordle's daily cycle makes completion shareable and repeatedly visible on outside networks; S19, C25 | Enthusiasts may want unlimited rounds | A shared challenge must itself be satisfying; his explanation is not a cadence experiment |
| Established reader preference vs. new-author survival | Consider the network and exposure for people with few followers; S20, C28 | Intrusion, relevance and trust for established readers | Measure both sides; he hypothesizes engagement gains, he does not supply Twitter experiment results |
| Audience growth vs. a founder's exit | Capacity, hit-driven decay and a personally meaningful payout can justify considering a sale; S19, C26 | Independent upside and autonomy | His claims about Wordle's stress and deal timing are speculation, not access to its founder's records |
| More features vs. an adjacent use case | Podcasts can extend an existing live-audio/creator ecosystem despite attention and market-size limits; S26, C29 | A narrower audience and a few dominant shows | Platform fit is an argument to test, not evidence the expansion worked |
| Cash purchase vs. conditional asset access | He reports exchanging equity for a domain with reversion on shutdown or pivot; S26, C30 | Dilution and the owner's lock-up cost | App-store discovery may not require the matching .com; the reported percentage is not a pricing rule |
| Owned assets vs. a new behavioral system | He questions the leap from NFT images in wallets to sustained game attention; S27, C31 | Funding and a holder community can supply resources but not every required behavior | Trace the unproven dependencies; do not invent numerical probabilities or treat the opinion as an observed outcome |
| Acquisition vs. durable use | Keep propagation and repeat value separate; S01/S06/S21 | A transient product may still have value | Judge against the actual business promise; object popularity need not retain the originating app |
| Quick wins vs. foundational work | He describes his own quick-win tendency and Musk's different horizon; S17 | Infrastructure, ownership and long-run reliability | This is a reported organizational contrast, not a proven optimal management system |

There is no supported universal ordering of these preferences or fixed numerical weighting. The repeated structure is to identify the constraint and the unit of value, then explain why one tradeoff makes sense in that context. This structure is inferred; the supporting passages remain attributable separately.

## Continuity and changes of view

| Comparison | What can be supported | What cannot be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder fear of copying → experience inside Facebook, S18 (2022) | He explicitly reports a changed perception of how quickly an incumbent responds | That incumbents never copy effectively or that the fear was irrational in every market |
| Twitter interest-graph explanation, S20 (2022) → curated Starter Packs, S17 (2026) | A similar activation problem appears in an earlier explanation and a later reported implementation | That the 2022 discussion caused the implementation, or that its metric proved durable user value |
| School-network invitations, S01/S02 → referral plus paid utility growth, S17 | Product type changes the distribution mechanism and acceptable growth economics | A reversal of a universal anti-paid-acquisition belief; the corpus does not establish such a belief |
| Skepticism about podcast scale + support for Twitter audio, S26 | Both views occur in one answer: market limits and platform fit can coexist | A contradiction that must be resolved by declaring one view his true belief |
| Frequent shipping, S24 → execution quality, S03/S01 | Feedback speed and credible execution answer different constraints | That one statement cancels the other or that speed always wins |
| Outline's 2013 government-product ambition → later consumer pivot recollection, S01/S17/S23 | The business direction changed; later accounts describe procurement disruption and founder fit | Which exact 2013 contract was canceled, complete financial performance, or the full decision timeline |

Repeated statements help identify a stable theme. Explicit first-person before/after accounts can support a change of view. Different roles, products or audiences usually support a context distinction; they do not by themselves establish an evolution in belief.

## Learning exercise

Choose a case and hide its choice and explanation. Write the goal, constraints, at least two plausible alternatives and the observation that would change your recommendation. Then reveal the source-backed explanation and compare the mechanisms, not the tone. Record where your own explanation differs and whether the source provides enough evidence to settle it.

For evaluation, previously published cases are learning material. They cannot be recycled as unseen tests after inclusion. See [fidelity-evaluation.md](fidelity-evaluation.md) for the frozen-version pilot and its limits.

## What is still missing

The public record is not a complete decision log. Prioritize material that supplies an absent link rather than another repetition of a slogan:

- **Failed products:** named alternatives, metrics before closure, costs, the reason to stop, and lessons that survived later attempts. Five's launch description alone supplies none of these outcomes.
- **Commercial decisions:** actual monetization, acquisition terms, buyer constraints and tradeoffs. A reported tactic or a personally meaningful exit does not establish a transferable business model.
- **X tenure:** fuller contemporary records across the entire tenure, responsibility boundaries and experiments rather than later retrospective claims alone.
- **Reliable attribution:** audio verification of bounded multiparty passages, missing replies/media, and access to the full paywalled interview through an ordinary authorized route.
- **Generalization:** fresh cases from different periods/products, repeated independent generations, human review, and product outcomes. Source count has no known conversion into thinking coverage.

Even exhausting discoverable public material cannot identify all private judgments: different internal models can explain the same selected public statements. A bounded reconstruction of documented reasoning can be improved and tested; complete access to a person's mind cannot be certified from this corpus.
